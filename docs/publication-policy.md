# Public publication policy

Being allowed to collect, store or display an item inside your organisation does not give you permission to
republish it to the public. Newsmon keeps these permissions apart, and public redistribution is denied unless it has
been granted explicitly.

## Six separate permissions

| Activity | Controlled by (per source in `sources.yaml`) | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Internal collection | `active`, `allowed_contexts`, robots.txt, attestation for customer sources | collect public feeds; customer sources inactive until attested |
| Internal storage | `storage`: `none` / `metadata` / `transient` / `full` | `metadata` (headline, link, times) |
| Internal display | `display`: `none` / `headline` / `snippet` / `full`, capped by any item restriction | `headline` |
| External AI processing | `ai_processing`, `external_ai_allowed`, `permitted_ops` | nothing is sent |
| Internal redistribution | `redistribution`, `authorised_users` (recipient gating is not built yet) | `none` |
| **Public redistribution** | `public_redistribution`: `none` / `metadata` / `headline`, plus a written `public_redistribution_basis` and `public_redistribution_url` | `none` |

Commercial redistribution, meaning selling or syndicating content, is outside Newsmon's scope and is never implied by
any flag.

## The public publication gate

`newsmon export-public` is the only path from the database to a public file. For each stored item
(`src/newsmon/publish/gate.py`):

1. The source's **current** `sources.yaml` row must grant `public_redistribution` and state its basis in words.
   Withdrawing the permission stops publication at the next export, and that source's earlier items are dropped from
   the rolling snapshot, including when collection fails and the previous snapshot is republished.
2. Customer-licensed content (`licence_owner: tenant`) can never be granted public redistribution. The configuration
   loader refuses it.
3. Every internal rule still applies. The item is refused if it is restricted, withheld, embargoed, withdrawn or
   killed, if it may not be displayed internally, or if its licence snapshot cannot be read.
4. A real item must carry an http(s) link to its publisher.
5. Only a closed list of fields is written: link, source name, publication and first-seen times, language, rule band
   and score, Newsmon's own topic and entity labels, duplicate count, and (at `headline` level only) the headline.
   Summaries, article bodies, AI output and licence details are never read into the public record.
6. `newsmon.publish.export.validate` rejects any file with an unexpected field, a non-http(s) link, a headline from a
   metadata-only source, or a mix of synthetic and real items. CI runs it again over the built website, together with
   a scan for credentials, local paths and text that only occurs in article bodies
   (`scripts/check_public_artifacts.py`).

Tests: `tests/integration/test_public_export.py`. They plant canary text in summaries, include sources without
permission, and check that neither reaches the output.

## What the project website publishes

| Dataset | Sources | Basis | Fields |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synthetic | `demo/config`: fictional wire items generated by `demo/make_demo_inbox.py` | written for this project; CC BY 4.0 | headline, times, labels (no links: there is no publisher) |
| Live | `demo/live-config`: NASA news releases | US federal works are not protected by copyright in the United States (17 U.S.C. 105), and NASA states its material is generally not subject to copyright. | headline, original link, times, labels |

These bases were read on 2026-11-25. Logos, seals, photographs and third-party material that may appear in the
releases are not published, because only headlines and links are. Public-domain status is a United States rule, and
reuse elsewhere may differ. NASA asks that its material not be used to imply endorsement. The dashboard's topics and
priority bands are Newsmon's own rule-based labels, not the publishers'.

Sources deliberately **not** used for the live dashboard, although they are publicly readable:

- U.S. Department of Justice news. Its content is in the public domain, but its headlines name people who are charged
  and not yet convicted. Republishing those names can raise defamation and privacy concerns that copyright status does
  not settle. In some jurisdictions, including Korea, even a true statement can be defamatory.
- commercial news sites and agencies, because their feeds are free to read, not free to republish;
- SEC, because its fair-access policy asks for a contact e-mail address in the User-Agent;
- U.S. Department of State and Department of Defense, which refused automated requests when checked;
- Federal Register, whose reuse terms could not be read by an automated client. It is a candidate once someone has
  checked them in a browser.

## Adding a live source

1. Find the publisher's own statement that permits public reuse of headlines and links. A feed being public, or no
   restriction being stated, is not enough.
2. Check robots.txt for the feed path and any User-Agent or rate rules.
3. Add the source to `demo/live-config/sources.yaml` with `public_redistribution`, the quoted basis and its URL.
4. Run `uv run python scripts/build_public_data.py live --out _build/data/live.json` and read the output.
5. Prefer sources whose headlines do not name private individuals in legal or otherwise sensitive contexts.

## Persistence between scheduled runs

No database is kept between website builds. Each run collects afresh, then merges the previously published
`data/live.json` (fetched from the website itself), keeping at most 300 items from the last 14 days. For each kept item, its source is
re-checked against the current permissions: a source switched to `none` disappears, and one switched to `metadata`
loses its headlines. An item that this run collected and the gate refused (for example because it is now withdrawn
or restricted) is not carried forward. An item withdrawn by its publisher that simply disappears from the feed cannot
be detected, and it ages out within 14 days. If collection fails, the previous snapshot is republished after the same
re-filtering. If that fails too, the site is built without live data. If
the whole build fails, nothing is deployed and the last good site stays online.
